THE QUESTION

When does explicit structure provide genuine evidence for trustworthy reasoning—and when does it merely create the appearance of reliability?

01 / ABOUT

Making model reasoning legible.

Correct answers do not necessarily come from reliable reasoning. Foundation models can produce convincing explanations that fail to faithfully reveal what influenced their outputs, or succeed only under a particular wording or representation.

SymTrust: Symbolic Methods for Trustworthy and Verifiable Reasoning explores how rules, constraints, programs, graphs, knowledge bases, proofs, tables, and diagrams can make reasoning easier to inspect, evaluate, and verify.

02 / TOPICS

A meeting place for structured thinking.

We welcome archival and non-archival work across methods, theory, datasets, benchmarks, evaluations, applications, position papers, and analyses of model failures.

  • Formal and structured reasoning traces
  • Neuro-symbolic and solver-augmented models
  • Faithfulness of structured explanations
  • Autoformalization and representation failures
  • Formal verification and external tools
  • Reasoning across language, visual, spatial, and symbolic modalities
  • Robustness across paraphrases and representations
  • Metrics for validity, calibration, and answer–trace consistency
  • Human use of structured explanations
  • High-stakes applications and negative results
